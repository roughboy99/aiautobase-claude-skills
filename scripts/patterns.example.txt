# Local safety-gate patterns - one .NET regex per line. Blank lines and #comments ignored.
#
# Copy this file to patterns.local.txt and replace the examples with YOUR private
# strings. patterns.local.txt is gitignored, so your real paths and hostnames never
# get published by the scanner that is supposed to protect them.
#
# The gate already ships with built-in patterns for generic credentials: provider
# token prefixes, assigned passwords, private key bodies, RFC-1918 IPs, JWTs, and
# credentials in URLs. You do NOT need to repeat those here.
#
# What belongs here is everything specific to you - the things no generic scanner
# could know to look for:
#
#   - absolute paths on your machines (both slash directions on Windows)
#   - internal hostnames and public IPs of servers you own
#   - your own domains, if they should not appear outside README/LICENSE/NOTICE
#   - personal email addresses
#   - internal project codenames or client names
#
# Escape regex metacharacters: a literal dot is \. and a literal backslash is \\.
# Prefix with (?i) for case-insensitive matching.

# --- Absolute paths (examples - replace with yours) ---
(?i)[A-Z]:[\\/]my-private-workspace
(?i)[A-Z]:[\\/]Users[\\/]myusername
/home/myusername\b

# --- Servers you own (examples - replace with yours) ---
\b203\.0\.113\.\d{1,3}\b
(?i)\bmy-internal-box\.local\b

# --- Your domains, if they should not leak (examples) ---
(?i)\bmy-private-domain\.com\b

# --- Personal email (examples) ---
(?i)[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+@my-private-domain\.com

# --- Client or project names under NDA (examples) ---
(?i)\bProjectCodename\b
